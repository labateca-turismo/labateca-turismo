Sister María Isaura Cardona Zapata Public Library

The town library is 42 years old. It was created by Municipal Agreement 007 of 19 November 1983, attached to the house of culture, with a donation of 1,500 books from the Ministry of Education. In 2004, by Agreement 019, it took the name of Sister María Isaura Cardona Zapata, in gratitude to the nuns of the Missionary Institute of Saint Thérèse, who spent 58 years teaching the children and young people of Labateca. It moved around town for years before it had a home: first the ground floor of the municipal building, then the lower end of the square — the old boys' school — then the old wing of the secondary school on carrera 3. In 2005 a proposal was put to the Embassy of Japan, it was approved, and on 17 September 2006 the present building opened in the Quinta Real neighbourhood, next to the community radio station and the shrine. It belongs to the National and Departmental Network of Public Libraries, is governed by Law 1379 of 2010, and provides the ten basic library services, including rural outreach with its 'travelling backpacks'. The photographs on this page are published by permission of the Municipality of Labateca (Resolution 322 of 25 August 2026).

Tip: It is a meeting place, not just a book store: it hosts the School of Artistic and Cultural Training in Literature, Writing and Oral Tradition, created by Agreement 008 of 2016, and three programmes on Morena FM Stereo come out of here — the Cultural News on Mondays, Literary Pills on Tuesdays and Story Factory on Thursdays. The library supported the booklet 'Labateca, break the silence: fragments of memory', in homage to the municipality's victims, and the booklet 'Redactando · Volcanes de Dios' from the University of Pamplona, which you can read in full in this site's digital library.
