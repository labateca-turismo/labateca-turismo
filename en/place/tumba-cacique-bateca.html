Tomb of Cacique Bateca

On the old royal road, some 3.6 km southwest of town, stands a stone-and-lime tomb that the people of Labateca know as the tomb of Cacique Bateca. The chamber is closed with an iron grate and, around the opening, cast metal plaques carry messages of thanks: 'I give thanks to Cacique Bateca for a favour granted.' This is not merely a relic: it is a living devotional site where people still leave flowers and plaques. The name is not invented: the records of the Royal Audiencia show that on 4 May 1560 the Oidor Tomás López questioned, in the lodgings at Labateca, 'Batecabala, cacique' of the town, held in encomienda by Captain Andrés de Acevedo, through an interpreter. That this tomb is his is local tradition, not a fact verified by archaeologists; what is certain is that the cacique's name was written into sixteenth-century papers and still lives in Labateca's memory.

Getting there
About 3.6 km southwest of the town center, along the old royal road. You reach the trailhead and from there it is roughly 260 metres of uphill walking (about 35 metres of climb) on a mule path.

Tip: Go with a guide or a neighbour who knows the royal road, and visit respectfully: for many families in Labateca this is a sacred site, not an attraction. Do not move or touch the plaques, the flowers or the grate. Pair the visit with the history of the Labateca pueblo de indios in the EL PUEBLO section.
