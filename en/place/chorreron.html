El Chorrerón Waterfall

A very tall waterfall dropping through the gorge between two mountains, within San Bernardo. From a distance it reads as a white thread against the green; up close it falls down a rock wall, throwing up a permanent mist. The arrival point is marked by two posts and a wooden crossbeam with the name carved into it, framed against the falls. The route was field-recorded with GPS and the track is drawn on the map.

Getting there
From Labateca it is about 35 minutes by vehicle to the Zambrano bridge, in San Bernardo: that is where the GPS route on the map begins, and as far as Google Maps will take you. From the bridge you continue on an unpaved track — about 10 minutes by motorbike — and the last stretch is on foot, some 20 minutes to the falls. Along the way you cross a plank-decked suspension footbridge. The track we recorded that day measures 8 km and +466 m of elevation gain from the bridge, but it includes detours and stops: anyone who knows the way does it in considerably less.

Tip: There is a sign along the way announcing an entry fee; the price box was blank on the day of the visit, and at the entrance by the Soma roadside stop there is a charge to pass, so bring cash. Wear shoes with grip — the last stretch is a rough path kept damp by the mist from the falls — and download the map before setting out, as there is no signal. Leave early: it's nearly two hours each way counting the drive. Soma and its hot springs are on the way, if you want to end the day there.
