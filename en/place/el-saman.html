The Samán Tree

A huge, very old samán tree standing at the roadside next to the bridge, its crown opening like an umbrella over the road with the river running below. Samán trees (Samanea saman, also called the 'rain tree') have some of the widest crowns in tropical America and can exceed 25 metres in height. This one is alive inside and out: bromeliads, orchids, ferns and Spanish moss grow along its branches like a hanging garden. Local tradition holds that it was planted at the same time as the samán of Bochalema —sown in 1888 and, according to that town's chroniclers, a child of the Samán de Táriba and grandchild of the Samán de Güere—, which would make this tree close to 140 years old. At its foot stand a wooden cross and planters made from yellow-painted tyres, which neighbours keep filled with flowers.

Getting there
About 2.7 km south-southeast of the town center, at the roadside next to the bridge. You can see it from the car: it is by far the largest tree around and impossible to miss.

Tip: Stand underneath and look up: bromeliads, orchids and Spanish moss grow along the branches. It makes a good stop and photo spot on the way out to the veredas. The tree's age comes from local oral tradition, not from written records.
