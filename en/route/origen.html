The Indian Town of 1623

Labateca was not founded: it was gathered.

The town's origin is not a Spanish founding with a charter and a square, but an order to gather thirteen Indigenous chieftaincies that already lived here into one place. This route follows what that decision left behind: the square where they gathered, the church that sealed it, the library holding the written memory and, 3.6 km along the old royal road, the tomb where townspeople still bring flowers to their cacique.

  • DurationHalf a day
  • DifficultyEasy in town · 3.6 km of unpaved road to the tomb
  • Starts atMain Square
  • WhenAny day. The library opens on school hours, so come on a weekday morning if you want to go in.
  • HowSelf-guided, using the site map
What you take away

The town you can walk across in twenty minutes is the result of a 1623 order that forced thirteen communities to live together. Four centuries later people still thank the cacique in writing.

Tip: Read the 1623 Auto de Población in the site's history section first: the route reads differently once you know what the document said. Bring water and closed shoes for the cacique's tomb.

The stops, in order

  1. Main stop

    Main Square

    Town center · Easy access · Starting point

    You are standing on the site the 1623 order chose. «Labateca» is a Chitarero word that the town translates as «God's volcanoes». Look around before entering the church: the square, the church and the steep streets are the mould imposed by colonial resettlement, repeated in dozens of Indian towns along this range.

    See the full entry · Get directions

  2. Stop

    Our Lady of Sorrows Parish

    In town center · Easy access · Mass: Mon–Sat 7 am & 6 pm · Sun 7 & 10 am, 3 & 6 pm

    The church put an official date on what already existed: in 1719 the site became the Parish of Our Lady of Sorrows. Look for the colonial baptismal font — the piece of furniture where the families' names were first written down — and the niche above the main altar, with the gold «milagros» devotees still offer.

    See the full entry · Get directions

  3. If you have time

    Sister María Isaura Cardona Zapata Public Library

    Beside the church, in the town centre · Free entry · easy access · Open during school hours · reading room, lending and free internet

    It opened in 1983 with 1,500 books donated by the Ministry of Education and carries the name of Sister María Isaura Cardona Zapata, in gratitude to the nuns who taught here for 58 years. It is where the town's oral memory became paper.

    See the full entry · Get directions

  4. Main stop

    Tomb of Cacique Bateca

    ~3.6 km from town · Easy · Open during the day

    The route ends here, and what does not fit in a document begins. On the old royal road, a tomb of stone and lime closed with an iron grate. Around the opening hang cast-metal plaques: «I thank Cacique Bateca for a favour received». This is not a ruin: it is a living place of devotion. The authority the 1623 order set out to replace is still being thanked four centuries later.

    See the full entry · Get directions
