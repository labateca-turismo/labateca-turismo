From the Royal Road to the Lirgua Waterfalls

A single road southwest links the cacique's tomb and the town's flagship waterfall.

Six kilometres of road west that hold the best the town has to offer outside its streets: Cacique Bateca's tomb on the old royal road and, further on, the two Lirgua waterfalls inside a property a family tends and works by hand. Halfway along lies La Peña, where you can sleep and turn the outing into two days.

  • DurationFull day · two days if you sleep at La Peña
  • DifficultyModerate · 1.6 km trail with stone steps, mud and a stream crossing
  • Starts atMain Square, heading southwest
  • WhenLirgua is open every day from 6:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Leave early: the trail is better walked in daylight and the property closes at five.
  • HowCan be done alone, better with a guide
What you take away

The same road the chroniclers called royal leads today to an Indigenous tomb that still receives flowers and to a waterfall a family maintains with hand-laid stone steps. The road is old; the care is present-day.

Tip: To enter Lirgua you must ask for the keys at the house by the bridge, and there is a per-person entrance fee; bring cash. It is private land tended by a family: leave no rubbish and respect the paths and gardens. Non-slip footwear is essential.

The stops, in order

  1. Stop

    Tomb of Cacique Bateca

    ~3.6 km from town · Easy · Open during the day

    3.6 km from town, on the old royal road, a tomb of stone and lime with an iron grate. The metal plaques around the opening are thank-yous: «I thank Cacique Bateca for a favour received». Pause before moving on: the road you are climbing is the one that appears in the chronicles.

  2. Main stop

    Lirgua and Dos Brazos Waterfalls

    ~6 km from town · Moderate · Every day, 6:00 a.m. – 5:00 p.m.

    Two waterfalls on the same stream. The first, Lirgua, drops high and strong into a rock pool; upstream, Dos Brazos splits into two parallel jets over a moss-covered wall. The trail between them runs 1.6 km and climbs 140 metres, from 1,620 to 1,760 m above sea level. The stone steps, gardens and hand-painted signs were made by the owning family: you are walking on someone's work.
