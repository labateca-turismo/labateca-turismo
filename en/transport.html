Getting here
and getting around

Labateca is reached by mountain road, and once you are here you move around by minibus, by pickup truck or on a motorbike. This is what to know before you set out.

Intercity

Getting to Labateca

From the three nearest cities. Times are for a normal run; in the rainy season the road can take longer.

  1. From Cúcuta

    ~113 km · 3 to 4 hours

    Buses and minibuses leave for Labateca from the Cúcuta bus terminal. There are two routes depending on the company: via Pamplona or via Toledo. Ask at the ticket window about the return time on the same day, because there is not always an afternoon service.

  2. From Pamplona

    The shortest leg

    Pamplona is the midpoint of the whole route. Minibuses and pickup trucks leave its terminal for Labateca and Toledo. It is also the prettiest stretch of the journey, through páramo landscapes.

  3. From Bucaramanga

    Changing at Pamplona

    Take a Bucaramanga → Pamplona bus, about four hours, and change there for Labateca. Set out early: the last service of the day to the municipality usually leaves in the afternoon.

🚗 If you are driving: the road is paved for most of the way. Fill up in Pamplona or Cúcuta and download the offline map before you leave, because there are stretches with no phone signal.

Around town and the veredas

Getting around Labateca

You can walk across the town centre end to end. For the rural veredas —and for the waterfalls, which are a long way from the square— you need transport, and there the people who know the road are in charge.

We are still gathering this part

We are collecting from the town's own drivers the details that actually help: which companies and which individuals run the service, which veredas they go up to, what time they leave, what they charge and what number to call. This is information that is published nowhere today and gets passed on by word of mouth.

We will publish it once it is confirmed with them, and not before. A wrong departure time is worse than none at all: it leaves someone standing on a road.

Do you drive, or do you know someone who does? If you provide transport in Labateca —minibus, pickup, motorbike taxi or charter runs to the veredas— and you want arriving visitors to know how to reach you, write to us. Appearing here costs nothing.

16 July

The drivers' day

The Virgin of Carmen is the patron saint of those who drive, and in Labateca it is a marked date. The buses, the pickups and the motorbikes are decorated and blessed. It makes complete sense: without them, nobody reaches this municipality and nobody leaves it.
