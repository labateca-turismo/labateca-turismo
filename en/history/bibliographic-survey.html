BIBLIOGRAPHIC SURVEY

Books on the history of Labateca and its region. Compiled within the project «Labateca, Volcanes de Dios». Prepared with the help of Gemini Notebook.

1 Introduction and regional context

This survey gathers and analyses the body of bibliography and historiography that supports the ancestral, colonial and republican past of the municipality of Labateca —known in its indigenous origins as «Volcanes de Dios», the volcanoes of God— and of the surrounding region in the old province of Pamplona. Its purpose is to give historians, scholars and cultural workers a rigorous base for preserving the region's memory.

Our history began a long time ago. Long stretches counted in millennia passed from the time our ancestors settled these beautiful Andean valleys, watered by three rivers, until the Spanish conquerors arrived. Reconstructing that requires setting the classic chronicles against modern monographs and archival research, to find where they agree and to clear away schoolbook myths that have travelled far.

2 Inventory of academic and research sources

What follows is a critical inventory of the seven main academic works that deal rigorously with the settlement, the encomienda system and the historical demography of Labateca and the old province of Pamplona.

3 Chronicles, memoirs and accounts

Besides the modern historiographical studies, Labateca's documentary heritage draws on three fundamental chronicles written by eyewitnesses or by churchmen in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

4 Three themes that run through the whole corpus

Read across one another, these documents bring out three large historiographical themes that shape the collective identity and territorial evolution of Labateca and its region.

A · Chitarero identity and pre-Hispanic resistance

The sources agree without exception that the pre-Hispanic populations of Labateca and Toledo belonged to the Chitarero people, known for their fine cotton weaving and for their use of gourds —which the conquerors called «chitareros». Against the schoolbook stereotype of a submissive pacification, the early judicial manuscripts prove a strong ethnic resistance to the seizure of reserve lands. Labateca's past speaks closely to that of the whole province of Pamplona, of which it was an indispensable demographic centre.

B · The brotherhood as the engine of economic and political autonomy

Studies of the colonial period show that the Brotherhood of Our Lady of Sorrows, founded on 4 July 1633 by thirteen indigenous chiefs, worked not only as the spiritual heart of the parish but as an institutional shield. The capital the brotherhood built up —community goods, cattle herds, maize fields and active loans made to landowners of the province— became the decisive legal support before the Royal Audiencia in 1778, allowing the indigenous community to appeal successfully and defeat the decrees of the crown attorney Francisco Antonio Moreno y Escandón, which had ordered the town demolished and its reserve lands sold.

C · Spanish-indigenous segregation and the separation of Toledo

The separation of Toledo from Labateca at the end of the eighteenth century (1790–1796) was deeply marked by the ethnic and social segregation between the incoming white farming population and the indigenous people of the Labateca reserve. While the indigenous community stayed in the old town centre protecting its doctrine chapel, the white and mestizo elites argued boundary disputes, damage to their crops by horses, and the physical instability of the Labateca bluff in order to justify erecting a parish at the site of Belén (El Salado — Toledo), administratively dismembering the original territory of the Valley of Sorrows.

5 Method and archival sources

To check and place the catalogued works, this survey relied on primary sources held in national and international repositories, chief among them:

National Archive of Colombia (AGN), Bogotá — Colonial sections: Inspections of Santander, Inspections of Boyacá, Reserves of Santander, Priests and Bishops.

Historical Archive of Pamplona (AHP-NS) — Notarial Protocol from the sixteenth to the eighteenth century, essential for tracking the rise of the sugar, wheat and coffee economies.

Parish Archive of Labateca (APL) — Book of Brotherhoods 001, a manuscript of 1633 holding the constitutions, the colonial inventories of ornaments and the minutes of the brotherhood's council.

Reading these against one another leads to one conclusion: rescuing Labateca's bibliographic heritage is not a matter of piling up stories, but a discipline that uncovers the material and institutional foundations on which the territory of the «Volcanes de Dios» was built.
